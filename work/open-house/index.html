Voula Evangelia [ concept · poster design · lettering · mockups ] Open House

OPEN HOUSE opens buildings that are usually closed to the public, for one weekend a year. These are my proposals for its “Design the Poster” competition. The starting point is the world of architecture — not a building, but the tools and the way of thinking behind one.

proposal 1

Drawn with the architect’s stencil

the idea

A tool every architect has held

The main element is a stencil ruler — the tool most architects have used for drawing by hand. The “Open House” lettering and the logo are cut out of it, alongside furniture, kitchen and bathroom fixtures that bring in a sense of home and tie the idea more closely to the word “house”.

the direction

Ready for anyone to draw

The stencil lies on millimetre paper, ready for whoever wants to create or draw. One system, two cities: orange on graph paper for Athens in April, yellow on blueprint blue for Thessaloniki in November.

the volunteers’ t-shirt

The print the stencil leaves

The design on the volunteers’ t-shirts shows the moment after: someone has picked up the stencil and left its print behind, as if they had traced the inside of every shape with a pencil. Uneven, hand-pressed edges — the same idea, now in use.

proposal 2

The key to the house

the idea

What opens a house

This proposal works with the words “Open House” themselves: the key is the symbolic tool that opens doors — and, with them, houses. During Open House, buildings and arcades that are usually closed, “locked” to the public, open up.

So the poster shows a key engraved with the “Open House” lettering, with the logo hanging beside it as a key-ring.

the volunteers’ t-shirt

A door left open

On the back, the key with its key-ring. On the front, discreetly, a minimal drawing of a door — a hint at the open nature of the buildings during the event. The front is shared by all; its colour follows the colour of the back.

proposal 3

A house to cut out and fold

the idea

Every house starts as a drawing

A second direction: the house as a paper cut-out, with fold lines, ready to be built — and filled with the crayon drawings of whoever lives in it. Below it, the “Open House” lettering is built from circles and blocks, like the parts of a model.
