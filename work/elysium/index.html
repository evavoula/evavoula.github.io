Elysium

Voula Evangelia [ concept · spatial composition · 3d model · unreal engine renders · film edit · sound ] Elysium

ELYSIUM is an experiential spatial installation that builds awareness of violence against women. It is not a building to look at, but a route you walk through: ten underground rooms, each one a form of abuse that most women will recognise — from an unwanted look to an unwanted touch.

It speaks to men and women alike, and takes apart the idea that gender-based violence is “a women’s issue”. It addresses victims, to help them speak, and perpetrators, to help them recognise what they do.

The twist: every visitor walks it in a role — as a victim or as a perpetrator — and the roles are reversed.

why

A violence that stays out of sight

Gender-based violence is so widespread that, if every woman reported it, the police could not handle the cases. Elysium was made in response to that rise — and to the indifference that surrounds it.

where

Inside a cliff on Folegandros

the site

Hidden, like the abuse itself

The installation sits inside a cliff near Lygaria beach, on the Cycladic island of Folegandros. The site was chosen on purpose: almost everything happens underground, out of sight — the way most of the abuse women live through stays hidden.

From the surface you only see a few soft, egg-like openings in the rock, and a small block where visitors come back up into the light.

how it works

Two roles, one route, ten rooms

the rules of the visit
  1. A role at the door. Visitors are split into victims and perpetrators — and the roles are reversed: men mostly enter as victims, since in a patriarchal world it is usually women who are. Anyone who says they have lived through abuse is not made to relive it.
  2. Two sides of every room. The rooms are built twice over: one side for the victims, one for the perpetrators. They walk the same route, on different paths.
  3. Points. Perpetrators earn points in every room, depending on how hard they make the victim’s experience. Some go all in; others step back, refuse, and simply watch.
  4. Two doors. At the end, after the two groups meet, the points decide. Under 200, the blue door leads out into the open air. Over 200, the red door takes you back to the start, to walk it all again — this time as the victim. Empathy is not explained; it is lived.

the rooms

Ten rooms, ten forms of abuse

01 — opening room

Everyone starts together

Inside the egg-shaped dome, under a single opening to the sky, among the banners of protests against femicide. This is the last moment everyone shares the same space — before the roles are handed out.

02 — unwanted attention

Being watched

A tunnel of screens playing news reports leads into a room of giant eyes that follow every move. Each perpetrator sits inside one of the eyes — a capsule they steer to keep a victim in sight. The longer they watch, the more points they score; a button opens and shuts the eye.

03 — catcalling · passage

Faces that speak to you

A narrow passage between angled panels. Huge faces lean in from both sides and speak as you squeeze through — there is no way to walk past them without hearing.

04 — walking alone during night

The way home

A long corridor in the dark, lit only by thin red lines, walls on either side. It is the street at night, the walk that feels longer than it is. After it, the victims reach a small red lounge — the first place they meet each other.

05 — spiked and drugged drinks

Losing your footing

A vast, almost black hall where distance and direction stop making sense — the disorientation of a drink you did not know had been spiked.

06 — unwanted touch

Nowhere to step aside

A narrow corridor with soft, elastic walls. From the other side, hands press through towards you, and the passage is too tight to avoid them.

07 — targeting of victims

Chosen

Darkness, and one cone of light. From their consoles, the perpetrators decide who stands in it.

08 — loss of self sense

On display

A white, spiralling room lined with life-size photographs of bodies — looked at, judged, voted on. You keep walking in circles, with less and less of yourself.

09 — solitude

Down, alone

Stairs, then a ladder inside a blue tube, down into a round, blue void. No one else is there.

10 — calling for help · art installation

The first domino

Victims and perpetrators finally meet, after the roles are revealed. Tall slabs stand like dominoes — the first step is awareness, and one falling slab moves the next. On the walls, red telephones play recorded calls for help.

the way out

More than 200

Two doors end the route. Less than 200 points: the blue door, up into the open air. More than 200: the red door — a lift that takes you back to the start, to walk the whole route again, this time as the victim.

the study model

Two paths through the rock

Before any drawing, a model of foil and wire: each lump a room, each wire a corridor. Drawn over it, the two routes that run through the whole project.

the name

Elysium, as satire

In Greek myth, the Elysian Fields were part of Hades — the final rest of heroes and of the virtuous. Naming a descent through abuse after them is a deliberate, bitter comment.

But it is also a promise: the experience is meant to be cathartic. Once you have gained awareness and empathy, you can set out on the path to a more virtuous life.
