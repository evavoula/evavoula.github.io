Voula Evangelia [ concept · installation · with konstantinos koinos ] 38780-118

THE TSEPELOVO WORKSHOP asked for an artistic, spatial work about a shell — and about how people live with, and inside, the natural world. It brought together students from different fields.

On an outing to the abandoned boarding house across from the Tsepelovo art station, we found a pile of old blankets. They became both the idea and the material of the work.

the idea

The last shell that is yours

the boarding house

A shell that is not enough

A boarding house is an outer shell. It covers the basic needs, but not the deeper, emotional ones: it is a shared space, full of rules and limits.

So the children build shells of their own, on the one place that belongs only to them — their bed.

the blanket

Warmth, comfort, safety

A blanket gives a feeling of warmth, comfort and safety. Wrapped around a body, it becomes a personal shell — a way to hide from the space outside, and to make a place where you feel at ease.

The work takes its name from the number marked on one of the blankets: 38780-118.

the shells

One blanket, one body, eight shells

Each blanket lies on the footprint of a bed, under a spotlight placed straight above it: the cold room, and the constant feeling of being exposed. Inside, the body shapes the blanket — folded flat, curled up, pulled over the head, standing.

the installation

Beds under spotlights

the workshop

Tsepelovo

living in nature

Shelters, old and new

A few days in the mountains of Epirus, among students from different fields, thinking about how people live with nature — and the oldest shelters we know.
